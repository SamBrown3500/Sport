The New Orleans Pelicans are a professional basketball franchise competing in the National
Basketball Association (NBA), based in New Orleans, Louisiana. The team traces its origins to
the 2002 relocation of the Charlotte Hornets to New Orleans, where it operated as the New
Orleans Hornets before rebranding to the Pelicans in 2013, adopting the name of Louisiana's
state bird, the brown pelican. The Pelicans play home games at the Smoothie King Center
and are members of the Western Conference's Southwest Division, though they have yet to
secure an NBA championship or advance beyond the conference semifinals despite multiple
playoff qualifications.
Key figures in the franchise's history include point guard Chris Paul, who led the team to its
first playoff series victory in 2011 by defeating the Los Angeles Lakers, and forward Anthony
Davis, whose dominant performances from 2012 to 2019 culminated in a 2018 Western
Conference Semifinals appearance before his contentious trade request prompted his
departure to the Los Angeles Lakers. The 2019 draft selection of power forward Zion
Williamson as the first overall pick initially sparked optimism, but persistent injuries have
limited his availability, contributing to the team's ongoing challenges in achieving sustained
contention amid a pattern of health issues affecting star players and organizational instability.

History
Origins as Charlotte Hornets and relocation to New Orleans
The Charlotte Hornets franchise originated as an NBA expansion team awarded to Charlotte,
North Carolina, in 1985, with operations commencing for the 1988–89 season under primary
ownership of George Shinn and a group of investors. The team played its home games at the
Charlotte Coliseum, achieving early success with playoff appearances in three of its first seven
seasons, including a franchise-best 54 wins in 1992–93, but attendance began declining amid
economic pressures and arena limitations by the late 1990s. Shinn's efforts to secure public
funding for a new arena failed, as local officials balked at taxpayer costs, exacerbating financial
losses reported at over $30 million in recent years.

By 2001, Shinn and co-owner Ray Wooldridge announced intentions to relocate due to
unsustainable operations in Charlotte, exploring options including Louisville, Memphis, and
Anaheim before selecting New Orleans, which offered incentives tied to the underconstruction New Orleans Arena (now Smoothie King Center). On May 10, 2002, NBA
owners approved the move by a 28–1 vote, with Portland's owner dissenting, allowing the
franchise to suspend Charlotte operations after the 2001–02 season. The team
rebranded as the New Orleans Hornets for the 2002–03 season, playing its first game on
October 30, 2002, a 91–79 win over the Houston Rockets, marking the NBA's return to New
Orleans since the Utah Jazz's departure in 1979. This relocation preserved the franchise's
continuity, including draft history and records, while Charlotte received a new expansion team
(the Bobcats, later renamed Hornets) starting in 2004–05.

Early years in New Orleans (2002–2005)
The New Orleans Hornets commenced their tenure in the city with the 2002–03 NBA season,
following the franchise's relocation from Charlotte, where it had played since its inception in
1988. The team's inaugural home game occurred on October 30, 2002, at the New Orleans
Arena, resulting in a 91–83 victory over the Chicago Bulls. Under head coach Paul Silas, the
Hornets achieved a 47–35 regular-season record, securing third place in the Central Division
and fifth in the Eastern Conference, which qualified them for the playoffs in their debut year in
Louisiana.
Key contributors included point guard Baron Davis, who averaged 20.1 points and 7.9 assists
per game, earning All-NBA Third Team honors, alongside forward Jamal Mashburn (21.0 points
per game) and center P.J. Brown (9.5 rebounds per game). During halftime of the first home
game, the franchise retired the No. 7 jersey of Pete Maravich, honoring his career with the
earlier New Orleans Jazz from 1974 to 1980. In the playoffs, the Hornets advanced to the first
round but fell to the Philadelphia 76ers in six games, with a 2–4 series outcome.
The 2003–04 season saw a coaching change to Tim Floyd, who guided the team to a 41–41
record, again finishing third in the Central Division despite Mashburn missing the first 44
games due to knee surgery. Davis continued as the offensive focal point, averaging 23.0 points
and 7.5 assists, while the team maintained defensive solidity, allowing 92.6 points per game.
They reached the playoffs once more but lost in the first round to the Miami Heat.
Injuries and roster instability marked the 2004–05 campaign under new head coach Byron
Scott, hired on May 28, 2004, yielding an 18–64 record—the league's worst—and fifth place in
the newly formed Southwest Division. Davis, limited to 35 games by knee and back issues, still
averaged 16.1 points and 6.4 assists when healthy, but the team's offense ranked last at
88.4 points per game amid widespread absences, including those of Mashburn and others.
This season concluded without playoff qualification, setting the stage for further challenges
ahead.

Chris Paul era and playoff contention (2005–2011)


The New Orleans Hornets selected Chris Paul fourth overall in the 2005 NBA draft out of Wake
Forest, marking the arrival of a franchise cornerstone amid the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina.
21 Due to the storm's devastation, the team relocated its home games to Oklahoma City's
Ford Center for the entire 2005–06 season and 35 games of the 2006–07 season, while
playing the remaining six home contests in New Orleans. Under head coach Byron Scott,
Paul's rookie campaign featured averages of 16.1 points, 5.1 rebounds, 7.8 assists, and 2.2
steals per game across 82 appearances, earning him unanimous NBA Rookie of the Year
honors and propelling the Hornets to a 38–44 record—a 20-win improvement from the prior
season's 18–64 mark.
In the 2006–07 season, Paul elevated his production to 17.3 points, 4.6 rebounds, 8.6 assists,
and a league-leading 2.4 steals per game, guiding the Hornets to a 49–33 record and their
first playoff appearance since 2004. However, they fell in the first round to the San Antonio
Spurs in four games. The 2007–08 campaign represented the era's pinnacle, with Paul
averaging 21.1 points, 4.0 rebounds, 11.6 assists (a league high), and 2.7 steals—securing AllNBA First Team and All-Defensive First Team selections—while the Hornets achieved a
franchise-record 56–26 mark, clinched the Southwest Division title, and earned the No. 2
Western Conference seed. In the playoffs, they swept the Dallas Mavericks 4–1 in the
first round before losing to the Spurs 4–1 in the conference semifinals; Paul's postseason
averages reached 22.0 points and 11.3 assists over 13 games.
The 2008–09 season saw Paul earn his second consecutive All-NBA First Team nod alongside
league-leading assists (10.7 per game) and steals (2.8 per game), but team injuries contributed
to a 49–33 finish and a first-round exit against the Denver Nuggets in five games (1–4 series).

Monty Williams assumed head coaching duties in June 2010, ushering the 2010–11 Hornets to
a 46–36 record and another playoff berth as the No. 7 seed. Paul delivered 15.9 points, 9.8
assists, and 2.5 steals per game in the first round, but the team fell to the Los Angeles Lakers
4–2. Over his six seasons, Paul's leadership transformed the Hornets into consistent
contenders, yielding four playoff appearances, 238 regular-season wins, and personal
accolades including six All-Star selections (2008–2011), four All-NBA honors, and multiple
assists/steals titles, despite roster and relocation challenges. His tenure concluded with
a blocked trade to the Lakers in December 2011, prompting his eventual move to the Los
Angeles Clippers.

Anthony Davis era and rebranding (2012–2019)

The New Orleans Hornets selected power forward Anthony Davis from the University of
Kentucky with the first overall pick in the 2012 NBA draft on June 28, 2012, following a draft
lottery win that secured the top selection despite holding the second-worst record from the
prior season. Davis debuted on October 31, 2012, and in his rookie 2012–13 season
under head coach Monty Williams, he played 64 games, averaging 13.5 points, 8.2 rebounds,
and 1.8 blocks per game while earning NBA All-Rookie First Team honors. The Hornets
finished 34–48, missing the playoffs amid a roster rebuild after Chris Paul's departure.
On January 24, 2013, owner Tom Benson announced the franchise's rebranding to the New
Orleans Pelicans, effective for the 2013–14 season, to foster stronger ties with the city's
identity and the state bird, the brown pelican; the change included new navy blue, red, and
gold colors replacing the prior teal and purple scheme. In the 2013 offseason, general
manager Dell Demps traded point guard Tyreke Evans and the draft rights to Nerlens Noel to
the Philadelphia 76ers for Jrue Holiday and Pierre Jackson, pairing an elite defender with Davis
to anchor the backcourt. The Pelicans' inaugural 2013–14 season yielded a 34–48 record,
hampered by injuries to Davis and Holiday, though Davis earned his first All-Star selection
while averaging 20.8 points and 10.0 rebounds in 66 games.
Davis emerged as a perennial All-Star and All-NBA performer from 2014 onward, leading the
league in blocks multiple times and finishing as high as third in MVP voting in 2014–15, when
he averaged 24.4 points, 10.2 rebounds, and 2.9 blocks en route to the Pelicans' first playoff
appearance since 2011—a first-round loss to the Golden State Warriors in five games.
Injuries plagued the 2015–16 season, resulting in a 30–52 finish and Williams' dismissal; Alvin
Gentry replaced him as head coach, emphasizing an up-tempo offense. The 2016–17
campaign ended 34–48, but the 2017–18 acquisition of injured center DeMarcus Cousins via
free agency formed a potent frontcourt duo with Davis, propelling the team to 48 wins and a
playoff sweep of the Portland Trail Blazers (Davis averaged 33.0 points and 11.5 rebounds);
they fell 4–1 to the Warriors in the semifinals.
Cousins' Achilles injury before the 2018–19 season contributed to a 33–49 record and another
playoff miss, despite Davis averaging 25.9 points, 12.0 rebounds, and 2.0 blocks in 75 games.

Zion Williamson era, injuries, and roster turnover (2019–present)

The New Orleans Pelicans selected Zion Williamson with the first overall pick in the 2019 NBA
Draft on June 20, following the trade of Anthony Davis to the Los Angeles Lakers on July 6,
2019, which netted the Pelicans Lonzo Ball, Brandon Ingram, Josh Hart, and multiple draft
picks. Williamson signed a four-year rookie scale contract on July 1, 2019. He missed
the first 44 games of the 2019–20 season after suffering a right knee meniscus injury during
preseason on October 21, 2019. Williamson debuted on January 22, 2020, scoring 22
points and grabbing 7 rebounds in a 121–117 victory over the San Antonio Spurs. In 24 games,
he averaged 22.5 points, 7.0 rebounds, and 2.1 assists per game, earning NBA All-Rookie First
Team honors, though the Pelicans finished 30–42 and missed the playoffs.
In the 2020–21 season, Williamson appeared in 61 of 72 games, earning his first All-Star
selection while averaging 27.0 points, 7.2 rebounds, and 3.7 assists per game. The team
posted a 31–41 record, again failing to reach the playoffs. His participation dropped sharply in
2021–22 after a right foot fracture diagnosed on October 21, 2021, leading to surgery on
December 21, 2021, and causing him to miss all 82 games. On July 1, 2022, Williamson signed
a five-year, $193 million maximum rookie extension with incentives potentially raising it to $231
million, but including performance-based guarantees tied to playing at least 41 games per
season for future years' security.
The 2022–23 season saw Williamson limited to 29 games due to a right hamstring strain,
averaging 26.0 points, 6.3 rebounds, and 4.6 assists. The Pelicans went 34–48. He rebounded
in 2023–24, playing 70 games and averaging 22.9 points, 8.8 rebounds, and 5.0 assists,
helping the team to a 49–33 record and a playoff berth, though they lost 4–0 to the Oklahoma
City Thunder in the first round after Williamson sat out the final regular-season games with a
hamstring issue. In 2024–25, a left hamstring strain sidelined him after November 6, 2024,
limiting him to early-season games and contributing to ongoing team struggles. Through six
seasons as of October 2025, Williamson has played in only 214 of 492 possible regularseason games, averaging 24.7 points, 7.2 rebounds, and 4.0 assists when active.
Roster turnover has been extensive amid repeated injury disruptions and lack of deep playoff
runs. Lonzo Ball departed in free agency after the 2020–21 season, signing with the Chicago
Bulls. On February 8, 2022, the Pelicans traded Josh Hart, Larry Nance Jr., Tomas Satoransky,
and a protected first-round pick to the Portland Trail Blazers for CJ McCollum, aiding a push to
the play-in tournament (36–46 record, eliminated). Brandon Ingram received a five-year, $158
million extension in 2021 but saw trade rumors persist. In the 2025 offseason, the Pelicans
traded both Ingram and McCollum, acquiring new veterans and draft picks to reshape the core
around a healthier Williamson, though his availability remains uncertain. The franchise
dismissed coach Alvin Gentry after 2020–21 and hired Willie Green, whose tenure has yielded
one playoff series win but persistent injury challenges.
