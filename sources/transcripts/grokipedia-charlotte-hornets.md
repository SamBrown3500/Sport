Inception and early development (1985–1992)
In 1985, entrepreneur George Shinn, a businessman from Kannapolis, North Carolina, began
efforts to bring an NBA franchise to Charlotte, assembling a group of investors to support his

bid amid the league's plan to expand by four teams. Shinn's group faced challenges in
securing local backing, as Charlotte's business elite initially viewed him as an outsider, but he
persisted in lobbying NBA officials. On April 5, 1987, NBA Commissioner David Stern awarded
the expansion franchise to Charlotte alongside the Miami Heat, with the teams set to
begin play in the 1988–89 season; the group paid a $32.5 million expansion fee to join the
league.
The franchise's name was selected through a public contest in 1988, where "Hornets"
emerged as the winner from over 2,000 entries, originally intended to be the Charlotte Spirit
but overridden by fan preference. The name drew inspiration from Charlotte's Revolutionary
War history, when British General Charles Cornwallis reportedly called the area a "hornet's nest
of rebellion" due to local resistance against British forces. This historical nod reflected the
city's spirited identity and helped build early community enthusiasm for the team.
The Hornets debuted in the 1988–89 season at the newly built Charlotte Coliseum, which
seated over 24,000 fans and became a hub for the franchise's early home games. The team
finished with a 20–62 record, one of the league's worst, reflecting the typical struggles of an
expansion squad building from scratch. Key additions included shooting guard Rex Chapman,
selected eighth overall in the 1988 NBA draft from the University of Kentucky, who provided
scoring punch as the franchise's first draft pick, and point guard Muggsy Bogues, acquired via
the 1988 NBA expansion draft, noted for his record-setting 5-foot-3 stature and defensive
tenacity.
The early 1990s saw incremental progress through strategic drafts that bolstered the roster. In
1990, the Hornets selected guard Kendall Gill fifth overall from the University of Illinois, adding
athleticism and perimeter defense to the lineup. The following year, they chose power forward
Larry Johnson first overall from UNLV, a versatile scorer who quickly became a cornerstone and
earned Rookie of the Year honors in 1991–92. These picks contributed to improved
performance, culminating in the franchise's first playoff appearance in 1993, where they upset
the Boston Celtics in the first round.
Shinn's ownership faced early challenges, including financial strains from the high expansion
costs and arena construction, which strained the investor group. By 1990, Shinn had bought
out his original partners, consolidating control but highlighting tensions over funding and
operations. Complaints about inadequate revenue streams, such as limited luxury boxes at the
Coliseum, surfaced as early as 1992, foreshadowing broader economic pressures on the young
franchise despite strong initial attendance averaging over 20,000 fans per game.

1990s prominence and relocation (1992–2002)
The Charlotte Hornets entered their most prominent era in the early 1990s with the selection
of Larry Johnson as the first overall pick in the 1991 NBA Draft, followed by Alonzo Mourning as

the top pick in 1992. This dynamic frontcourt duo powered the team to a franchise-best 44Sign in
38 record in the 1992-93 season, securing the Hornets' first playoff appearance and victory in
franchise history by defeating the Boston Celtics 3-1 in the Eastern Conference First Round.
However, they fell 1-4 to the New York Knicks in the Conference Semifinals. Johnson, known
for his athleticism, finished second in the 1992 NBA Slam Dunk Contest, captivating fans with
powerful dunks that highlighted his role as the team's emerging star. The pairing of Johnson
and Mourning, both future All-Stars, drew significant attention but was marred by reported
tensions, contributing to the franchise's shift in direction after three seasons together.
Tensions culminated in a blockbuster trade on November 3, 1995, when the Hornets sent
Mourning, Pete Myers, and Leron Ellis to the Miami Heat in exchange for Glen Rice, Matt
Geiger, Khalid Reeves, and a 1996 first-round draft pick. Rice, a sharpshooting forward,
immediately elevated the offense, earning All-Star selections in 1996, 1997, and 1998 while
averaging over 20 points per game each season. Under coach Dave Cowens, the Hornets
achieved their pinnacle in 1996-97 with a 54-28 record—the best in franchise history—
despite a first-round sweep by the Knicks (0-3). The following year, bolstered by power
forward Anthony Mason (acquired via trade from the Knicks in August 1996), they posted a 5131 mark and advanced past the first round before losing 1-4 to the Chicago Bulls. Mason
earned All-NBA Third Team honors that season for his defensive contributions.
The late 1990s and early 2000s saw continued competitiveness, highlighted by the third
overall selection of point guard Baron Davis in the 1999 NBA Draft. Davis emerged as a key
leader, leading the Hornets to a 53-29 record in 1999-2000 (lost 1-3 to the Philadelphia 76ers
in the first round) and playoff appearances in 2001 and 2002. The season was marred by
tragedy when longtime Hornets guard Bobby Phills died in a car accident on January 18, 2000,
deeply affecting the team and leading to the retirement of his No. 13 jersey. In 2000-01,
under coach Paul Silas, they swept the Heat 3-0 before falling 3-4 to the Milwaukee Bucks in
the Conference Semifinals. The 2001-02 season brought another 44-38 finish, a 3-1
upset over the Orlando Magic, and a 1-4 Conference Semifinals loss to the New Jersey Nets.

Financial strains and arena disputes plagued owner George Shinn's tenure, with declining
attendance at the aging Charlotte Coliseum exacerbating losses amid failed negotiations for
public funding of a new facility. In January 2002, the Hornets announced plans to relocate,
selecting New Orleans after commitments for luxury suites and season tickets. On May 10,
2002, NBA owners approved the move by a 28-2 vote, allowing the franchise to begin play as
the New Orleans Hornets in the 2002-03 season and effectively ending the original Charlotte
era.

The Bobcats era (2004–2014)

Following the relocation of the original Charlotte Hornets to New Orleans in 2002, the NBA
approved an expansion franchise for Charlotte, which began play as the Bobcats in the 2004–
05 season under majority owner Robert L. Johnson, the founder of Black Entertainment
Television and the league's first Black majority owner. The team, priced at $300 million for
the expansion fee, made its debut at the Charlotte Coliseum with a roster assembled via the
2004 NBA expansion draft. In their inaugural campaign, the Bobcats finished with an 18–64
record, the worst in the Eastern Conference, under coach Bernie Bickerstaff. Center Emeka
Okafor, selected second overall in the 2004 NBA draft, provided a bright spot by averaging
15.1 points and 10.9 rebounds per game, earning him the NBA Rookie of the Year award.
Michael Jordan, a Charlotte native and NBA legend, joined the ownership group as a minority
stakeholder in June 2006, acquiring a 10% share as part of an investment in Johnson's
portfolio, and gradually increased his influence, including roles in basketball operations. By
March 2010, Jordan had purchased a majority stake for $275 million, becoming the first former
player to hold controlling interest in an NBA franchise. Under his leadership, the Bobcats
emphasized draft selections to build the core, including acquiring forward Gerald Wallace in
the 2004 expansion draft, who became a defensive standout and two-time All-Star during his
tenure from 2004 to 2011. In 2011, with the ninth overall pick, they drafted guard Kemba
Walker out of UConn, who quickly emerged as the team's scoring leader.
The Bobcats achieved their first playoff berth in the 2009–10 season, finishing 44–38 and
earning the seventh seed in the Eastern Conference, but they were swept 4–0 by the Orlando
Magic in the first round. Four years later, in 2013–14, they returned to the postseason with a
43–39 record as the seventh seed, only to suffer another first-round sweep, this time 4–0
against the Miami Heat, led by LeBron James. These appearances marked the franchise's
limited postseason success amid ongoing challenges.
The Bobcats endured significant hardships, most notably in the 2011–12 lockout-shortened
season, when they posted a 7–59 record—the worst winning percentage (.106) in NBA history
—prompting widespread criticism and a push from ownership and fans to rebrand the
struggling franchise. Despite talents like Walker and Al Jefferson, who joined via free agency
in 2013, the team hovered near the bottom of the league standings for much of the era,
reflecting difficulties in roster construction and competitive consistency.

Revival and Kemba Walker years (2014–2020)
Following a decade of poor performance under the Bobcats name, including multiple seasons
with fewer than 20 wins, the franchise underwent a transformative rebranding in 2014,
reverting to the original Charlotte Hornets moniker for the 2014–15 NBA season. The NBA
Board of Governors unanimously approved the change in July 2013, after owner Michael
Jordan, who had acquired a controlling interest in 2010, initiated discussions to reclaim the
name following the New Orleans Pelicans' rebranding. As part of the agreement, the

Hornets regained the original franchise's history and records from its inaugural era (1988–
2002), integrating them into the team's official legacy while the Pelicans retained only their
records from 2002 onward. Jordan, a basketball legend and the team's principal owner,
further increased his ownership stake in 2014, solidifying his role in steering the franchise's
revival.
Kemba Walker, selected ninth overall by the Bobcats in the 2011 NBA Draft, emerged as the
cornerstone of the revitalized Hornets, serving as the franchise's leading scorer and emotional
leader through consistent high-volume performances. Under his guidance, the team
showed signs of competitiveness, qualifying for the playoffs in the 2013–14 season—still
operating as the Bobcats—with a 43–39 record, though they were swept 4–0 by the Miami
Heat in the first round. The 2015–16 campaign marked a high point, as the Hornets posted a
48–34 record and advanced as the Eastern Conference's sixth seed, pushing the Heat to a
decisive Game 7 before a 4–3 series defeat. Walker's impact peaked during this period,
earning him consecutive NBA All-Star selections from 2017 to 2019, including a fan-voted
starting nod in 2019.
To complement Walker's scoring, the front office pursued roster upgrades, trading for versatile
forward Nicolas Batum from the Portland Trail Blazers in June 2015 in exchange for Gerald
Henderson and Noah Vonleh. Batum signed a five-year, $120 million extension in July 2016,
forming a dynamic backcourt duo with Walker that emphasized playmaking and defense.
The Hornets further bolstered their frontcourt by acquiring Dwight Howard via trade from the
Atlanta Hawks in June 2017, adding veteran rim protection and rebounding for the 2017–18
season. However, post-2018 challenges mounted, with injuries sidelining key contributors like
Walker (who missed 17 games in 2018–19 due to knee issues) and Batum (limited to 58 games
in 2018–19 by shoulder and elbow ailments), contributing to three straight non-playoff finishes
despite records hovering around .500.
Walker's tenure concluded in the 2019 offseason when the Hornets traded him to the Boston
Celtics on July 6, 2019, receiving point guard Terry Rozier, a protected first-round pick, and two
second-round picks in return. The ensuing 2019–20 season proved tumultuous, shortened
by the COVID-19 pandemic after the league suspended play on March 11, 2020, following 65
games for Charlotte. The Hornets limped to a 23–42 record, finishing ninth in the Eastern
Conference and out of playoff contention, as the roster adjustment period and ongoing injury
concerns hampered consistency.

LaMelo Ball era and rebuilding (2020–present)
The Charlotte Hornets entered a new phase of rebuilding in the 2020 NBA Draft by selecting
guard LaMelo Ball with the third overall pick, a choice that marked a shift toward youth
development and high-upside talent acquisition. Ball, who had played professionally
overseas before entering the draft, quickly emerged as a cornerstone, earning the NBA Rookie

of the Year award for the 2020–21 season after averaging 15.7 points, 5.9 rebounds, and 6.1
assists per game in 51 appearances. The team finished that season with a 33–39 record,
showing flashes of potential but missing the playoffs for the fifth straight year.
The following season, 2021–22, saw improved results with a 43–39 mark, driven by Ball's AllStar caliber play, though the Hornets again fell short of postseason qualification. However,
the franchise faced mounting challenges thereafter, including persistent injuries to key players.
Ball suffered recurring ankle and wrist issues, limiting him to just 36 games in 2022–23 and 22
in 2023–24, while forward Miles Bridges dealt with a hip injury late in 2023–24 and a right
knee bone bruise that sidelined him for 10 games in 2024–25. These setbacks
contributed to declining performance: 27–55 in 2022–23, 21–61 in 2023–24, and a leagueworst 19–63 in 2024–25, extending the playoff drought to a decade.
Significant organizational changes punctuated the era, beginning with the rehiring of head
coach Steve Clifford in June 2022 for a second stint, during which he compiled a 61–107
record before stepping down in April 2024. Clifford was replaced by Charles Lee, a
longtime NBA assistant, who was hired on May 9, 2024, to instill a fresh defensive identity in
the young core. Off the court, majority owner Michael Jordan sold his controlling stake in
June 2023 to a group led by Gabe Plotkin and Rick Schnall, with the transaction finalized in
August 2023 for an estimated $3 billion; Jordan retained a minority interest. The new
ownership prioritized long-term rebuilding, exemplified by the 2023 draft selection of forward
Brandon Miller at No. 2 overall, who averaged 17.3 points as a rookie and provided scoring
versatility alongside Ball. In the 2024 NBA Draft, the Hornets selected French forward Tidjane
Salaün sixth overall, further bolstering the young roster. Despite these moves, the
Hornets continued to prioritize development over immediate contention, with no playoff
appearances from 2020 through 2025.
In the 2025–26 season, under head coach Charles Lee, the Hornets have shown signs of
progress in their rebuilding efforts, compiling a 36–34 record as of March 20, 2026, ranking
10th in the Eastern Conference and 4th in the Southeast Division. The team has averaged
116.0 points per game, 46.1 rebounds per game, and 26.6 assists per game, while shooting
46.2% from the field and 37.2% from three-point range. Brandon Miller has led the team in
scoring with 20.2 points per game, Moussa Diabate in rebounding with 8.7 rebounds per
game, and LaMelo Ball in assists with 7.5 assists per game. Defensively, the Hornets have a
Defensive Rating of 115.1 (16th in the NBA) and allow 112.5 points per game (10th), with
particular strength in rebounding defense—allowing the fewest opponent rebounds per game
at 39.5 (1st) and ranking second in defensive rebound percentage at 76.8%—but weakness in
forcing turnovers, ranking 27th in opponent TOV% allowed at 11.5%, along with 7.1 steals and
4.6 blocks per game. Notable highlights include a 9-game winning streak earlier in the season
