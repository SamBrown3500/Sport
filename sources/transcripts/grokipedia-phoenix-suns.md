1968–1976: Formation and Initial Development
The Phoenix Suns were established as an NBA expansion franchise in 1968, alongside the
Milwaukee Bucks, to expand the league to 17 teams for the 1968–69 season. A group of
investors including Karl Eller, Don Pitt, Don Diamond, and Richard Bloch secured the franchise
rights for Phoenix, which had competed against cities like Milwaukee, Kansas City, and
Cleveland for one of the two available slots. Jerry Colangelo, then 28 years old and
previously with the Chicago Bulls, was appointed as the team's first general manager, marking
the youngest such hire in league history at the time. The Suns selected players in the NBA's
expansion draft on April 15, 1968, acquiring veterans like forward Dick Van Arsdale and center
Paul Silas to form the core of the inaugural roster.

Under head coach Johnny "Red" Kerr, the Suns played their home games at the Arizona
Veterans Memorial Coliseum and launched their season on October 18, 1968, with a 101–98
victory over the Seattle SuperSonics, setting an NBA record for the most points scored by an
expansion team in their debut quarter with 41. Van Arsdale scored the franchise's first
basket on a layup. Despite the promising start, the team struggled with inexperience and
finished the 1968–69 season with a 16–66 record, the worst in franchise history up to that
point. In the 1969 NBA Draft, the Suns selected center Neal Walk with the second overall
pick, adding size to the frontcourt, though Walk's career was hampered by injuries.
The 1969–70 season showed improvement, with the Suns achieving a 39–43 record and
qualifying for their first playoffs as the Western Division's fourth seed. Kerr coached the first
38 games (15–23 record) before being fired; Colangelo then served as interim head coach,
leading the team to a 24–20 finish and a first-round playoff loss to the Los Angeles Lakers in
seven games (3–4 series). Colangelo transitioned back to general manager, hiring Jerry
Pardee as coach, but the 1970–71 season yielded a dismal 20–62 mark amid ongoing roster
instability. Key acquisitions included drafting guard Paul Westphal in the second round
of the 1972 NBA Draft (ninth overall pick traded from Boston), who emerged as a scoring
threat.
By the mid-1970s, the Suns began building momentum through strategic drafts and trades. In
1973, they drafted forward Curtis Perry, bolstering the frontcourt, and in 1975, acquired guard
Rick Roberson. The 1975–76 season under coach John MacLeod marked a turning point,
with a 42–40 record and another playoff appearance, defeating the Seattle SuperSonics in the
first round before falling to the Golden State Warriors. This period laid the groundwork
for sustained competitiveness, as Colangelo focused on player development and fan
engagement in Phoenix's growing market, transitioning from expansion novelty to a viable NBA
contender.

1976–1992: Building a Competitive Foundation
Following their appearance in the 1976 NBA Finals, the Phoenix Suns, under managing general
partner Jerry Colangelo, focused on roster continuity and strategic acquisitions to sustain
competitiveness. Colangelo, who had served as general manager since the franchise's
inception in 1968, prioritized developing homegrown talent and making calculated trades to
bolster the backcourt and frontcourt. The team retained core players from the Finals run,
including center Alvan Adams and guard Paul Westphal, while head coach John MacLeod
implemented a disciplined, team-oriented system that emphasized balanced scoring and
defensive rebounding.
In the 1977 NBA Draft, the Suns selected forward Walter Davis fifth overall, who quickly
emerged as a scoring threat, averaging 16.9 points per game as a rookie and earning AllRookie First Team honors. Davis's addition complemented Westphal, an All-Star guard who

averaged 20.3 points and 4.8 assists in the 1977-78 season, helping the Suns achieve a 49–
33 record and advance to the Western Conference Finals, where they fell to the Seattle
SuperSonics in seven games. The franchise posted winning records in 11 of the 16 seasons
during this period, reflecting organizational stability amid league expansion and increased
competition.
The Suns made 10 playoff appearances between 1977 and 1992, including Western
Conference Finals trips in 1979, 1981, and 1987, though they were eliminated each time by
eventual conference champions. Key to this consistency was the 1987 draft, where Phoenix
selected power forward Armon Gilliam second overall, providing interior scoring with 18.5
points and 7.0 rebounds per game in his debut season. Trades also proved pivotal; in February
1988, the Suns acquired guard Kevin Johnson from the Cleveland Cavaliers in exchange for a
first-round pick, injecting speed and playmaking into the lineup—Johnson averaged 20.2
points and 10.2 assists in his first full season with Phoenix in 1988-89.
Coaching transitions marked the later years, with MacLeod departing after the 1986-87
season following 14 years and 696 wins, the most in franchise history at the time. Cotton
Fitzsimmons took over in 1988, guiding the team to a 55-27 record in 1989-90 and a
conference semifinals berth. By 1992, the Suns had assembled a veteran core capable of
contending, setting the stage for future success, though injuries and tough matchups limited
deeper postseason runs. Paul Westphal, who retired as a player in 1984 after six seasons with
Phoenix where he earned three All-Star selections, returned as an assistant before assuming
head coaching duties in April 1992.

1992–2004: Peak Eras with Barkley and Nash
On June 17, 1992, the Phoenix Suns traded Jeff Hornacek, Tim Perry, and Andrew Lang to the
Philadelphia 76ers for All-Star power forward Charles Barkley. Under head coach Paul
Westphal, Barkley's arrival transformed the team, leading to a franchise-record 62 wins in the
1992–93 regular season. Barkley earned NBA Most Valuable Player honors, averaging 25.6
points, 12.2 rebounds, and 5.1 assists per game. The Suns advanced through the Western
Conference playoffs, defeating the Los Angeles Lakers 4–2 in the Conference Finals, before
losing to the Chicago Bulls 4–2 in the NBA Finals; a highlight was their triple-overtime victory
in Game 3 on June 13, 1993.
The Suns maintained strong regular-season performances in the following years under
Westphal, posting 56 wins in 1993–94 and losing in the Western Conference Semifinals to the
Houston Rockets, then 59 wins in 1994–95 before a first-round sweep by the San Antonio
Spurs. The 1995–96 season saw a decline to 41 wins and another first-round exit to the
Spurs, amid Barkley's suspension for conduct issues, marking the end of his tenure with
Phoenix after four seasons. Westphal was fired mid-season in February 1996, with Danny
Ainge assuming head coaching duties.

In the 1996 NBA Draft on June 26, the Suns selected point guard Steve Nash 15th overall from
Santa Clara University. Nash backed up veteran Kevin Johnson, averaging 3.3 points in
1996–97 as the team finished 40–42 and missed the playoffs for the first time since 1987–88,
then 5.8 points in 1997–98 when Phoenix rebounded to 56 wins but lost in the first round 3–2
to the Spurs. On June 24, 1998, the Suns traded Nash to the Dallas Mavericks for the
draft rights to forward Pat Garrity.
Post-Nash trade, the Suns under coaches Ainge and later Scott Skiles achieved playoff berths
in shortened 1998–99 (33–17 record, Western Semifinals loss to Spurs) and full seasons
1999–2000 (53–29, Western Semifinals loss to Lakers), 2000–01 (51–31, first-round loss to
Kings), 2001–02 (49–33, first-round loss to Kings), and 2002–03 (44–38, first-round loss to
Spurs), but failed to advance beyond the second round. The team slumped to a 29–53
record in 2003–04 under Frank Johnson, missing the playoffs and setting the stage for Nash's
return in free agency that offseason.

2004–2012: High-Octane Offense and Transitions
The Phoenix Suns entered a dynamic era in 2004 following the return of point guard Steve
Nash via sign-and-trade from the Dallas Mavericks, pairing him with coach Mike D'Antoni to
pioneer a fast-paced, high-octane offense emphasizing quick shots within seven seconds of
possession. This system, built around Nash's playmaking, Amar'e Stoudemire's interior
scoring, and Shawn Marion's versatility, propelled the Suns to the league's highest scoring
average of 110.2 points per game in 2004–05 while posting a franchise-record 62–20 regularseason mark, earning the top seed in the Western Conference. Nash captured the NBA
Most Valuable Player Award that season, averaging 15.5 points and a league-leading 11.5
assists per game. In the playoffs, the Suns defeated the Memphis Grizzlies and Los Angeles
Clippers before falling to the San Antonio Spurs 4–1 in the Western Conference Finals.
The offensive revolution continued through 2007–08, with the Suns maintaining elite pace
and scoring efficiency despite defensive vulnerabilities and injuries. They recorded 54–28
in 2005–06, advancing to the Western Conference Semifinals where they were swept 4–0 by
the Dallas Mavericks amid controversial officiating claims; 61–21 in 2006–07, losing 4–2 to the
Spurs in the semifinals; and 55–27 in 2007–08, suffering a first-round upset loss to the Spurs
4–3 despite holding a 2–1 series lead. Stoudemire's 25-game suspension early in 2007–08
for violating the NBA's anti-drug policy involving testosterone further hampered the roster,
contributing to inconsistent defense and postseason failures against the Spurs dynasty.
D'Antoni resigned after the 2007–08 season, citing frustrations with roster limitations and
management decisions, leading to the hiring of Terry Porter as head coach for 2008–09.
Porter's tenure emphasized defensive improvements and slower tempo, but the Suns
struggled with a 18–33 record by the All-Star break in 2008–09, prompting his midseason
dismissal. Assistant Alvin Gentry assumed interim duties, finishing 18–13 and guiding a

partial offensive resurgence, which earned him the permanent role. Gentry reinstated
elements of the up-tempo style in 2009–10, yielding a 54–28 record and another Western
Conference Finals appearance, where they lost 4–2 to the Los Angeles Lakers; Nash averaged
17.8 points and 11.0 assists. However, free-agent departure of Stoudemire to the New York
Knicks that offseason, combined with aging core players like Nash and Grant Hill, signaled
decline.
Subsequent seasons marked a transition to rebuilding, as the Suns posted a 40–42 record in
2010–11 and 33–33 in the lockout-shortened 66-game 2011–12 campaign, missing the
playoffs both years amid injuries to Nash and lack of depth. Gentry's teams averaged below
.500 from 2010–12, reflecting roster attrition including the 2008 trade of Marion to the Miami
Heat (later rerouted to Toronto) and failure to secure a superstar complement. Nash, the
era's linchpin with two MVPs and leadership in 422 games (2004–12), departed as a free
agent to the Los Angeles Lakers in July 2012, closing the high-octane chapter and ushering in
a prolonged rebuild.

2012–2020: Rebuilding and Drafting Booker
The Phoenix Suns initiated a rebuilding effort following the 2011–12 season's 25–47 record
under Alvin Gentry, who was dismissed on January 18, 2013. Lindsey Hunter assumed interim
duties, compiling a 5–16 mark to close out the year, resulting in an overall 25–57 finish and the
franchise's worst winning percentage since its inaugural campaign. Jeff Hornacek was
appointed head coach on June 7, 2013, leading the team to a 48–34 record in 2013–14—their
first winning season since 2009–10—but they fell short of the playoffs by one game in the
Western Conference. The following year, the Suns regressed to 39–43 amid injuries and
inconsistency, before plummeting to 23–59 in 2015–16, prompting Hornacek's firing on April
23, 2016.
Earl Watson was promoted from assistant to head coach on April 25, 2016, overseeing backto-back seasons of 24–58 in 2016–17 and 21–61 in 2017–18, both among the league's worst
records. Watson was relieved of duties on October 31, 2017, with assistant Jay Triano finishing
the 2017–18 campaign. Igor Kokoskov debuted as head coach in 2018–19, guiding the Suns to
a 19–63 record, tying the NBA's lowest win total that year. Monty Williams replaced him on May
3, 2019, yielding a 34–39 mark in the 2019–20 season, interrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic
and concluded in the NBA Bubble without postseason qualification. These years featured
frequent front-office turbulence under owner Robert Sarver, including the hiring of Ryan
McDonough as general manager in 2013 and his dismissal in 2018, succeeded by James
Jones.
Central to the rebuild was the 2015 NBA Draft, where the Suns selected guard Devin Booker
13th overall on June 25, 2015, from the University of Kentucky. Despite securing the league's
worst record (39–43) in 2014–15, unfavorable lottery odds positioned Phoenix at No. 13, a

outcome criticized for the NBA's draft system disadvantaging the most deficient teams.
Booker debuted on October 28, 2015, and in his rookie 2015–16 season, averaged 13.8 points,
2.5 rebounds, and 2.6 assists over 76 games, earning NBA All-Rookie Second Team
recognition as the Suns' lone bright spot amid their 23–59 collapse. His scoring prowess
emerged immediately, highlighted by a 38-point outburst against the Cleveland Cavaliers on
March 24, 2016.
Booker's ascent continued, averaging 22.1 points per game in 2016–17 and eclipsing 25 points
in subsequent seasons, establishing him as the franchise cornerstone despite organizational
instability. The Suns supplemented the core with drafts like center Alex Len (5th overall, 2013),
forward T.J. Warren (14th, 2014), forwards Dragan Bender (4th) and Marquese Chriss (8th) in
2016, Josh Jackson (4th, 2017), and center DeAndre Ayton (1st, 2018), alongside trades such
as acquiring Bogdan Bogdanović rights in 2014 and dealing point guard Goran Dragić to Miami
on February 18, 2015, for future assets. Yet persistent losing—yielding an 11-year playoff
absence—underscored draft misfortunes and developmental hurdles, with Booker remaining
the rebuild's enduring success amid a 147–413 record (.263 winning percentage) from 2012–
13 to 2019–20.

2020–2023: Revival with Paul and Ownership Change
The Phoenix Suns acquired veteran point guard Chris Paul from the Oklahoma City Thunder on
November 16, 2020, in a trade that sent Kelly Oubre Jr., Ricky Rubio, Ty Jerome, Jalen Lecque,
and a 2022 first-round pick (top-14 protected) to Oklahoma City. Paul's arrival provided
playmaking stability to a young core featuring Devin Booker and Deandre Ayton, ending the
Suns' 10-year playoff absence under head coach Monty Williams, who had been hired in May
2019.
In the 2020–21 season, the Suns compiled a 51–21 regular-season record, securing the No. 2
seed in the Western Conference. They advanced to the NBA Finals for the first time since
1993, defeating the defending champion Los Angeles Lakers 4–2 in the first round, sweeping
the Denver Nuggets 4–0 in the conference semifinals, and overcoming the Los Angeles
Clippers 4–2 in the conference finals, before falling to the Milwaukee Bucks 4–2. The
Finals appearance highlighted Paul's impact, as he averaged 21.8 points and 8.2 assists per
game in the postseason.
The Suns built on this momentum in 2021–22, posting an NBA-best 64–18 regular-season
record and claiming the Western Conference's top seed. However, they were eliminated in
the conference semifinals by the Dallas Mavericks 4–2, hampered by Paul's temporary
absence due to a broken right thumb sustained after the All-Star break.
The 2022–23 season saw further roster evolution with the February 9 trade for Kevin Durant
from the Brooklyn Nets, but injuries plagued the team, including Paul's left hand fracture in

Game 1 of the first-round playoffs against the Nuggets. The Suns finished 45–37, the No. 4
Western seed, and lost to Denver 4–2 in the first round. Concurrently, owner Robert Sarver
faced a league investigation into workplace misconduct, resulting in a one-year suspension
and $10 million fine in September 2022; he agreed to sell the franchise on December 20,
2022, to a group led by Mat Ishbia for a record $4 billion, with the deal finalized on February 6,
2023. Ishbia's takeover introduced aggressive spending and operational shifts,
signaling a push for contention despite the playoff setbacks.

2023–Present: Superteam Assembly and Performance Struggles
On February 9, 2023, the Suns acquired Kevin Durant and T.J. Warren from the Brooklyn Nets
in exchange for Mikal Bridges, Cam Johnson, Jae Crowder, four unprotected first-round draft
picks (2023, 2025, 2027, 2029), and a 2028 first-round pick swap. This move,
orchestrated by owner Mat Ishbia shortly after his February 2023 purchase of the franchise,
paired Durant with Devin Booker and aimed to elevate the team to contention following their
2021 NBA Finals appearance.
In the 2023 offseason, the Suns further assembled their core by trading Chris Paul, Landry
Shamet, multiple second-round picks, and cash to the Washington Wizards for Bradley Beal on
June 18, while sending Eric Gordon, Judson Williams, and a 2023 second-round pick to the Los
Angeles Clippers as part of the deal. Additional signings included Drew Eubanks, Keita
Bates-Diop, Chimezie Metu, and Yuta Watanabe on multi-year contracts to bolster depth.
The team hired Frank Vogel as head coach in June 2023 after dismissing Monty Williams,
emphasizing defensive structure to complement the offensive firepower of Booker, Durant,
and Beal.
The 2023–24 season yielded a 49–33 record, securing the sixth seed in the Western
Conference, with the Suns ranking 10th in points per game (116.2) but 13th in defensive points
allowed (113.2). However, injuries hampered the trio—Durant missed 27 games, Beal sat out
29—exposing bench weaknesses and chemistry issues, as the team struggled with a 7–15
stretch midseason. In the playoffs, the Suns were swept 4–0 by the Minnesota
Timberwolves in the first round, scoring under 100 points in two games and failing to adapt to
Minnesota's length and defense.
Following the playoff exit, the Suns fired Vogel on May 9, 2024, and hired Mike Budenholzer as
head coach in June 2024 to instill championship habits from his Milwaukee Bucks tenure.
The 2024–25 season deteriorated to a 36–46 record, finishing 11th in the West and missing
the playoffs, with a league-worst 27th-ranked defensive rating of 117.7 amid ongoing injury
woes and poor team cohesion.
In response, the Suns dismissed Budenholzer and appointed Jordan Ott, a Cleveland Cavaliers
assistant, as head coach on June 4, 2025, marking their fourth coach in four years under

Ishbia. The 2025 offseason saw the superteam's dissolution: Durant was traded to the
Houston Rockets in a multi-team deal yielding Jalen Green, Dillon Brooks, and draft rights to
Khaman Maluach (No. 10 pick), among others; Beal was waived in July to gain financial
flexibility. These moves shifted focus to retooling around Booker with younger assets,
though early 2025–26 results remained pending as of October 2025.
