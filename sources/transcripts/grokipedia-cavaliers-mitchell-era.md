# History of the Cleveland Cavaliers (Grokipedia), section "2022–present: Donovan Mitchell era

2022 trade and playoff return
On September 3, 2022, the Cleveland Cavaliers acquired star guard Donovan Mitchell from the Utah Jazz
in a blockbuster trade that reshaped the franchise's trajectory. In exchange, Utah received forward Lauri
Markkanen, guard Collin Sexton, rookie guard Ochai Agbaji, three unprotected first-round draft picks (in
2025, 2027, and 2029), and two pick swaps (in 2026 and 2028). This move integrated Mitchell into
the young core anchored by point guard Darius Garland, forward Evan Mobley, and center Jarrett Allen,
creating a dynamic frontcourt and backcourt combination poised for contention.
The 2022–23 regular season marked a breakthrough for the revamped Cavaliers, who posted a 51–31
record and clinched the No. 4 seed in the Eastern Conference. Mitchell thrived in his debut
campaign with Cleveland, averaging a career-high 28.3 points per game across 68 appearances and
earning selection to the All-NBA Second Team for his scoring prowess and leadership. The team's
balanced attack and defensive intensity, bolstered by the new core, propelled them to 51 wins—their
highest total since the 2016–17 season—and returned them to playoff relevance after a four-year
absence.
In the playoffs, however, the Cavaliers faltered in the first round against the New York Knicks, dropping
the series 1–4 despite entering as the higher seed. Cleveland's offense, which had ranked among the
league's most efficient during the regular season, sputtered under playoff pressure, averaging just 94.2
points per game in the series. Key injuries compounded the issues, with Mobley limited by a lingering
left ankle sprain that hampered his mobility and production, as he averaged only 9.8 points and 10.0
rebounds over the five games.
The trade's immediate impact transformed the Cavaliers into legitimate Eastern Conference contenders,
signaling the end of their post-LeBron rebuild and setting the stage for sustained success under head
coach J.B. Bickerstaff. Bickerstaff, credited with fostering team chemistry and tactical growth, finished
fourth in NBA Coach of the Year voting for guiding the young roster to a franchise-best regular-season
mark since 2010.

2023–2024 deep run
The Cleveland Cavaliers finished the 2023–24 regular season with a 48–34 record, securing the No. 4
seed in the Eastern Conference and avoiding the play-in tournament for the second consecutive year.

particular, excelled defensively, earning a spot on the NBA All-Defensive Second Team with 1.7 blocks per
game and helping limit opponents to the league's seventh-lowest points per game allowed at 110.2.
Entering the playoffs, the Cavaliers faced the fifth-seeded Orlando Magic in the first round, a matchup
marked by gritty, low-scoring battles that showcased Cleveland's improved perimeter defense. The
series went the full seven games, with the Cavaliers rallying from an 18-point halftime deficit in Game 7

to win 106 94 on May 3, 2024, behind Mitchell s 39 points and Jarrett Allen s 20 rebounds. This victory
marked the franchise's first playoff series win since 2018, igniting widespread fan enthusiasm in
Cleveland, where attendance at Rocket Mortgage FieldHouse surged during the postseason and local
media highlighted the team's resilience as a sign of emerging contention. The Cavaliers' defensive
prowess was evident throughout, holding Orlando to under 90 points in four of the seven games,
including a 97–83 Game 1 triumph.
In the Eastern Conference semifinals, the Cavaliers met the top-seeded Boston Celtics but fell in five
games, 4–1, as Boston's balanced attack overwhelmed Cleveland's frontcourt. The series began with
lopsided losses in Games 1 (120–95) and 2 (118–94), where the Celtics exploited Cleveland's fatigue from
the first round. The Cavaliers stole Game 4, 93–87, with strong interior defense from Mobley and
Allen restricting Boston to 37.5% shooting, but the Celtics closed it out 113–98 in Game 5 on May 15,
2024, advancing to the conference finals. Despite the exit, the deep run validated the young core's
growth, with Mitchell averaging 28.2 points per game in the playoffs and the team's defensive rating
ranking among the top three during the postseason.

2024–2025 record season and semis loss
The 2024–25 Cleveland Cavaliers season marked a pinnacle of regular-season success under new head
coach Kenny Atkinson, who was hired on June 28, 2024, following the departure of J.B. Bickerstaff.
The team compiled a franchise-record 64–18 mark since the 1991–92 campaign, securing the No. 1 seed
in the Eastern Conference and leading the league in wins during the post-LeBron James era.
Atkinson's emphasis on pace, ball movement, and defensive continuity propelled the Cavaliers to the
NBA's top offensive rating (121.9 points per game) while maintaining a stout defense (12th in points
allowed at 112.4). The squad's depth shone through a robust bench unit that provided consistent
scoring and energy, with no major injuries disrupting the core rotation throughout the year.
Individual accolades underscored the team's dominance. Atkinson earned the NBA Coach of the Year
award on May 5, 2025, recognized for transforming Cleveland into a 15-game winning streak starter early
in the season—the fourth such occurrence in NBA history. Guard Donovan Mitchell, the offensive
engine, was selected to the All-NBA First Team after averaging 28.1 points, 5.3 rebounds, and 6.2 assists,
capping a career-high efficiency season. Forward-center Evan Mobley claimed the Defensive Player
of the Year honor on April 24, 2025, anchoring the paint with 2.1 blocks per game and elevating the
team's defensive versatility built on prior gains.
In the playoffs, the Cavaliers swept the eighth-seeded Miami Heat 4–0 in the first round, highlighted by
a 138–83 blowout in Game 3 that stands among the most lopsided postseason victories. However,
their Eastern Conference semifinals run ended in disappointment with a 1–4 defeat to the Indiana
Pacers, marred by offensive stagnation—Cleveland shot under 40% from the field in three losses and
struggled with perimeter creation against Indiana's pressure defense. The series exposed
vulnerabilities in sustaining rhythm beyond the regular season, despite the team's overall 5–4 playoff
record.

2025–present early success
The Cleveland Cavaliers entered the 2025–26 NBA season building on their record-setting 64–18

campaign from the prior year, which ended in an Eastern Conference semifinals loss to the Indiana
Pacers. As of November 19, 2025 (prior to the game against the Houston Rockets), the team holds a 10–
5 record, placing them second in the Eastern Conference and second in the Central Division. This
strong start featured a high-octane offense, highlighted by a franchise-record streak of four
consecutive games scoring at least 128 points, including a 148–115 outburst against the Washington
Wizards on November 7 and a 128–122 comeback victory over the Chicago Bulls on November 8. The
Cavaliers' scoring average reached 122.3 points per game through early contests, ranking sixth leaguewide, driven by their league-leading 17.0 three-pointers made per game. Recent games include a loss to
the Toronto Raptors (126–113 on November 13), a win over the Memphis Grizzlies (November 15), and a
118–106 victory over the Milwaukee Bucks on November 17, along with a 146–134 win over the Minnesota
Timberwolves on January 10, 2026, in which five players—Donovan Mitchell (28 points), Evan Mobley (24),
Jaylon Tyson (23), Darius Garland (22), and Sam Merrill (20)—each scored at least 20 points, tying the NBA
record for the most such performers in a single game and marking the second occurrence in franchise
history. On November 18, the NBA fined the Cavaliers $100,000 for resting Donovan Mitchell and
Evan Mobley in the November 12 win over Miami.

A healthy core has fueled this early momentum, with guards Donovan Mitchell and Darius Garland both
fully recovered and contributing significantly; Mitchell averaged 30.4 points per game through midNovember, while Garland provided playmaking support without reported setbacks. The frontcourt duo
of Evan Mobley and Jarrett Allen remained elite, combining for dominant rebounding and rim protection—
Mobley posted 18.9 points, 1.0 steals, and 1.7 blocks per game—bolstering a defense that ranked seventh
in steals per game at 9.5. Roster enhancements via free agency and trades added depth, including the
July 2025 signing of Larry Nance Jr. to a one-year deal and the acquisition of Lonzo Ball via trade from the
Chicago Bulls in exchange for Isaac Okoro, alongside the February 2025 trade for forward De'Andre
Hunter from the Atlanta Hawks in exchange for Caris LeVert, Georges Niang, and draft assets; Hunter has
emerged as a key scorer at 17.4 points per game. No major injuries have disrupted the lineup, allowing
consistent rotations and fostering team chemistry.
Under head coach Kenny Atkinson, the Cavaliers continued emphasizing versatile defensive schemes
that prioritize steals and transition opportunities, while focusing on rotational depth to sustain
performance through the grind of the season and into the playoffs. This approach has kept Cleveland in
contention for the Eastern lead, with milestones like a 98-game streak of at least 10 three-pointers
made—setting an NBA record—and Mitchell's career-high 15-rebound performance underscoring their
balanced attack. Fan sentiment reflects heightened optimism for a deep postseason run, potentially
culminating in Finals contention, buoyed by the team's resilience in close games and avoidance of the
injury woes that plagued previous years.
Looking ahead, the Cavaliers positioned themselves as contenders to defend their status as a top seed,
with early successes signaling potential for another 60-win pace if health holds. The renewed rivalry with
the Pacers, intensified by last season's playoff clash where Indiana eliminated Cleveland in five games (1–
4 series), added intrigue to upcoming matchups, promising fierce Central Division battles.
